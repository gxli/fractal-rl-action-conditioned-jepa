\section{What this experiment asks}
This follow-up asks a simple question: does a JEPA prediction window roughly
as wide as the agent's visual field improve learning or frozen-encoder
transfer? The agent moves through a $512\times512$ periodic pink-noise
landscape, starts at its global potential minimum, and is rewarded for
distance travelled. It sees a world-frame $32\times32$ local terrain patch
plus its motion state.

Actions are held for four 20-ms physics integrations, so one decision lasts
80 ms. A $K=128$ JEPA target therefore spans 10.24 s, or about 30.7 pixels at
3 px/s: nearly one 32-pixel field of view. Every curve uses 20k decisions
(80k physics steps), and each band is the variation across four deterministic
held-out terrain seeds.

The source terrain is $\alpha=3$; frozen transfer changes only the terrain
texture to $\alpha=2$ or $\alpha=3.5$. We compare four arms: separate-encoder
SAC; shared critic-trained SAC; shared SAC+JEPA; and \emph{shared
critic-trained SAC+JEPA+SIGReg}. In all three shared arms, the critic TD loss
updates one common visual encoder and the actor reads a stop-gradient copy of
that latent. SIGReg is therefore an added JEPA regularizer on the same shared
encoder, not a separate encoder design.

\section{What the learning curves show}
All four source runs and all eight frozen-transfer runs are complete. SIGReg
finishes at $3.065\pm.050$ on source, $3.093\pm.026$ at $\alpha=2$, and
$3.071\pm.038$ at $\alpha=3.5$. It is therefore close to the shared-SAC
control at $\alpha=2$ (3.090), but below it at $\alpha=3.5$ (3.090). In
short: SIGReg is competitive on the harder $\alpha=2$ shift, but it does not
produce a consistent final-speed advantage. The verdict is:
\begin{itemize}
\item Critic-trained sharing is the reliable improvement over separate SAC.
\item Long-horizon JEPA is competitive, but does not improve the final-speed
      asymptote consistently.
\item SIGReg hurts frozen-transfer generalization overall: it is roughly tied
      with shared SAC at $\alpha=2$, but is lower on the $\alpha=3.5$ shift
      and below the shared-SAC control in aggregate.
\end{itemize}
