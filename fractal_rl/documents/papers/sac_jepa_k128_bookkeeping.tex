\section{Design and task}
The benchmark is designed to make representation learning testable without
turning RL into an underpowered sparse-reward search problem. First, the
fractal potential produces connected basins, slopes and acceleration fields:
the agent must infer how its throttle and steering interact with local terrain
and damping, rather than classify a static image. Second, reward is dense
distance travelled (with a small control cost), so every transition supplies a
learning signal to actor and critic. This combination makes the task physically
non-trivial while remaining trainable in short controlled sweeps on one RTX
3090; improvements can therefore be attributed to representation and transfer
rather than simply to surviving an extremely sparse objective.
\subsection{Terrain game and observation}
Each task is a $512\times512$ periodic pink-noise landscape. A standardized
Gaussian intensity field $I$ is squared to form the potential $\phi=I^2$;
the spectral exponent $\alpha$ changes the spatial correlation and basin
geometry while preserving the intended pointwise scale. The source is
$\alpha=3$ and frozen transfer changes only $\alpha$ to 2 or 3.5. The point
mass begins at the sampled global potential minimum, observes a world-frame
$32\times32$ potential patch plus velocity, speed, mass and previous action,
and is rewarded for distance travelled minus a small control cost.

\begin{figure}[ht]\centering
\includegraphics[width=.9\linewidth]{dumps/sac_noim_analysis/terrain_value_histograms_white.png}
\caption{Reference terrain statistics. The potential marginal is comparable
across exponents; $\alpha$ changes the spatial texture and connectivity that

The dynamics use 20 ms integration, maximum forward/lateral accelerations
of 2/1, terrain-force scale 4, and linear damping .6. The repeat-4 policy
holds an action for four integrations ($.08$ s); each 40 s episode therefore
contains 500 decisions. Critic-trained sharing means TD loss updates a common
visual encoder while the actor consumes a stop-gradient latent.
\section{Objective and controlled comparison}
Let $\epsilon$ be white Gaussian noise. The periodic fractal intensity is
formed in Fourier space by $I=\mathcal F^{-1}[\mathcal F(\epsilon)w(f)]$,
where $w(f)=(\|f\|^2+f_c^2)^{-\alpha/4}$ and the DC component is removed.
After centering and unit-variance normalization, the conservative terrain
potential is $\phi(x,y)=I(x,y)^2$. The field acceleration is
$a_\phi=-4\nabla\phi/g_{.95}$, where $g_{.95}$ is the 95th-percentile
gradient magnitude for that terrain. With action $a=(a_\parallel,a_\perp)$,
\[\dot v=2a_\parallel\hat v+a_\perp\hat v_\perp+a_\phi-.6v.\]
SAC uses TD regression and entropy-regularized policy improvement,
\[L_Q=\|Q-r-\gamma(\min_i\bar Q_i-\eta\log\pi)\|^2,\qquad
L_\pi=\mathbb E[\eta\log\pi(a|o)-\min_iQ_i(o,a)].\]
JEPA predicts an EMA future latent from the applied action sequence,
\[L_{\rm JEPA}=1-\cos(q([z_t,a_t,\ldots,a_{t+K-1}]),\operatorname{sg}[\bar z_{t+K}]).\]
For shared-critic arms, $z=e(o)$ is updated by $L_Q$; the policy reads
$\operatorname{sg}[z]$. Thus critic TD gradients shape the visual encoder,
while actor gradients cannot destabilize it. The JEPA arm adds $.025L_{\rm JEPA}$;
inverse, margin, variance and distributional regularizer terms are zero.
\subsection{Prediction scale}
Does a JEPA prediction interval close to the $32$ px encoder FOV improve
transfer beyond critic-trained sharing alone? The repeat-4 decision interval is
$.08$ s, so $K=128$ is 10.24 s and about 30.7 px at 3 px/s. This is deliberately
longer than K=32 (7.7 px) and K=64 (15.4 px), while remaining comparable to one
visible field of view.

\section{Completed results}
All three primary source runs and all six baseline frozen-transfer runs
completed. The SIGReg source ablation is also complete, and its matched
frozen-transfer runs are in progress. The curves
separate the architecture effect from the long prediction task: shared vanilla
SAC is the control for critic-first sharing; the JEPA arm differs only by the
K=128 action-conditioned prediction objective.

\paragraph{Early transfer.} On the harder frozen $\alpha=2$ shift, JEPA is
best initially: at 2k/4k/6k decisions it reaches 1.091/1.635/2.160, versus
shared vanilla SAC at .707/1.339/2.042 and separate SAC at .506/.624/1.179.
By 20k it does not retain an endpoint advantage (JEPA 3.068; shared vanilla
3.090; separate 3.079). Thus K=128 supports faster immediate transfer, not a
higher final asymptote. On $\alpha=3.5$, early curves are close and no robust
JEPA advantage is visible.
\section{Conclusion}
The four completed source arms converge to similar final speeds: long-horizon
JEPA, including the SIGReg ablation, does not raise the asymptote in this
reactive distance-maximization task. The transfer
curves reveal the useful distinction. Critic-trained sharing supplies the
large early-transfer improvement over separate SAC because TD loss learns a
value-relevant visual encoder quickly. On the harder $\alpha=2$ shift, K=128
JEPA further improves the first 2k--6k decisions by predicting action
consequences across roughly one FOV. Thus sharing is the primary sample-
efficiency mechanism; long-horizon JEPA is an additional immediate-transfer
signal when the terrain shift has more complex local structure.

\section{Dense landscape UMAPs}
The representation probe is not fitted only to rollout samples. At each of
2k, 6k, 10k, 14k and 20k decisions, it evaluates every point of a fixed
$128\times128$ world-location grid on one held-out $\alpha=3$ terrain. Every
grid point supplies a complete $32\times32$ visual FOV and the same fixed
proprioceptive state. cuML fits a three-dimensional UMAP to the dense states;
the corresponding deterministic rollout is then overlaid on the potential,
latent-RGB landscape, and UMAP cloud. Thus the background represents the
landscape available to the agent, while the line identifies the states it
actually visited.

These are critic-trained shared-encoder architectures. Consequently Actor,
$Q_1$, and $Q_2$ have the same encoder output by construction; the report
therefore shows the one non-duplicated shared representation per method.
